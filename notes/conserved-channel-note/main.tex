\begin{abstract}
In the proposed duality between double-scaled SYK at infinite
temperature and the 't~Hooft model [arXiv:2607.05678], the meson tower
is to be read from correlators of singlet bilinears; we study their
Majorana form $A_n=\sum_i\psi_i(\ad_H)^n\psi_i$. We prove two statements at strict
double scaling ($N=\infty$, every $\lambda$). First, the two-flavor
($i\neq j$) part of every $A_n$ channel cannot propagate. Its chord image
is a polynomial in the chord Hamiltonian, by a three-term recursion with
scalar coefficients, so every spectral moment $\mu_{k\ge1}$ vanishes at
every coupling and matter weight. Second, this does not freeze the
channel. The flavor-diagonal part is of the same order, and the
flavor-summed $A_2$ is exactly a conserved multiple of $H^2-1$ plus a
matter chord of dimension two:
$A_2-\langle A_2\rangle=\tfrac{2\lambda(1-q)}{1+q}(H^2-1)+\nu\,O_2$, so
the propagating part has width $2(1-q^2)$ at every $\lambda$. The proof
is a sign-reversing involution on chord diagrams. For $3\le n\le6$ the
same construction gives a polynomial in $H$ plus matter chords of
dimension $2,\dots,n$, verified in exact computer algebra but not
proved. Each statement is checked against exact finite-$N$ arithmetic
and exact diagonalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and statement}

Double-scaled SYK ($N$ Majorana fermions, $H=i^{p/2}\sum J\,\psi^p$,
$\lambda_{\rm chord}=2p^2/N$ fixed, $q=e^{-\lambda_{\rm chord}}$; we write
$\lambda\equiv\lambda_{\rm chord}$ throughout) is solved
at $N=\infty$ by chord combinatorics \cite{BINT,BM}. Ensemble-averaged
traces reduce to amplitudes on the chord Hilbert space
$\{\,|n\rangle\,\}_{n\ge0}$, with the chord Hamiltonian acting (in the
formal basis, units $\langle H^2\rangle=1$) as
\begin{equation}
T\,|n\rangle=|n+1\rangle+[n]_q\,|n-1\rangle,
\qquad [n]_q=\frac{1-q^n}{1-q}.
\end{equation}
Traces below are normalized, $\Tr\mathbf{1}=1$. A matter operator of
dimension $\Delta$ opens a matter chord; while it is
open the Hamiltonian acts by the one-particle transfer matrix $T_M$ on
states $|n_0,n_1\rangle$ ($n_0$ H-chords opened before the matter chord,
$n_1$ after):
\begin{equation}
T_M\,|n_0,n_1\rangle=|n_0,n_1{+}1\rangle+[n_1]\,|n_0,n_1{-}1\rangle
+q^{\Delta+n_1}[n_0]\,|n_0{-}1,n_1\rangle ,
\label{eq:TM}
\end{equation}
the three terms being: open a new H-chord; close one opened after the
matter chord; close one opened before it (crossing the matter chord, weight
$q^{\Delta}$, and the $n_1$ later chords, weight $q^{n_1}$)
\cite{BINT,BM}. The matter chord itself opens and closes via
\begin{equation}
\op\,|n\rangle=|n,0\rangle,
\qquad
\cl\,|n_0,n_1\rangle=q^{\Delta n_1}\,|n_0+n_1\rangle .
\end{equation}

For flavors $i\neq j$, the ensemble average of a \emph{sequential} pair
word factorizes through the zero-chord sector,
\begin{equation}
\Big\langle\Tr\big[\,H^{a}\psi_i H^{b}\psi_i\,H^{c}\psi_j H^{d}\psi_j\big]
\Big\rangle_J
=\langle0|\;T^{a}\,C(b)\;T^{c}\,C(d)\;|0\rangle,
\qquad
C(m):=\cl\circ T_M^{\,m}\circ\op ,
\label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta=\Delta_\psi$ the single-fermion weight ($\Delta_\psi=1/p$ for
the leading chord weight; nothing below depends on its value). We call
$C(m)$ the \emph{dressed propagator}: it is the two-point insertion
$\psi\,H^m\,\psi$ seen from the zero-chord sector.

The channels of interest are the singlet bilinears of \cite{MSS2607},
\begin{equation}
A_n=\sum_i\psi_i(\ad_H)^n\psi_i,
\qquad
(\ad_H)^n\psi=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^r\binom{n}{r}H^{\,n-r}\psi H^{\,r}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:factor} and linearity, inserting $A_n$ (per flavor pair) acts
on the chord line as
\begin{equation}
\widehat A_n=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^r\binom{n}{r}\,C(n-r)\,T^{\,r}
\;=\;\frac{d^n}{dt^n}\Big[\,C(t)\,e^{-tT}\Big]_{t=0},
\qquad C(t):=\cl\,e^{tT_M}\op ,
\label{eq:Ahat}
\end{equation}
and the spectral moments of the pair channel are
\begin{equation}
\mu_k \;=\; \big\langle\Tr\big[(\ad_H)^k(X)\,Y\big]\big\rangle_J
\;=\;\langle0|\,\ad_T^k\big(\widehat A_n\big)\,\widehat B_n\,|0\rangle ,
\label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
where $X=\psi_i\,(\ad_H)^n\psi_i$, $Y=X^\dagger$ on flavor $j$, and
$\widehat B_n$ is the chord image of the $Y$ block (its form is irrelevant
below). Our result:

\begin{theorem}[conserved channels]
$C(m)$ is a polynomial in $T$ for every $m$. Hence $\widehat A_n$ is a
polynomial in $T$, $\ad_T^k(\widehat A_n)=0$, and every moment
\eqref{eq:moments} of the $i\neq j$ pair amplitude vanishes for every
$n\ge1$ and every $k\ge1$ --- at every $q\in[0,1)$ and every matter
weight $\Delta$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The recursion}

For $j\ge0$ define the $j$-fold closes
\begin{equation}
\cl_j\,|n_0,n_1\rangle
=[n_1][n_1{-}1]\cdots[n_1{-}j{+}1]\;q^{\Delta(n_1-j)}\,|n_0+n_1-j\rangle ,
\qquad \cl_0=\cl ,
\end{equation}
(zero if $n_1<j$).

\begin{lemma}[three-term recursion, scalar coefficients]
\begin{equation}
\cl_j\circ T_M
=q^{\Delta+j}\;T\circ \cl_j
\;+\;[j]_q\;\cl_{j-1}
\;+\;\big(1-q^{2\Delta+j}\big)\;\cl_{j+1}.
\label{eq:rec}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply both sides to $|n_0,n_1\rangle$ and match the two chord-number
sectors. Write $[n_1]^{(j)}=[n_1]\cdots[n_1{-}j{+}1]$ and
$G=n_0+n_1$. Raising sector ($|G-j+1\rangle$): the left side receives only
the open term of \eqref{eq:TM}, giving $[n_1{+}1]^{(j)}q^{\Delta(n_1+1-j)}$;
the right side gives $q^{\Delta+j}[n_1]^{(j)}q^{\Delta(n_1-j)}$ from
$T\,\cl_j$ plus $c_j\,[n_1]^{(j-1)}q^{\Delta(n_1-j+1)}$ from $\cl_{j-1}$.
Using $[n_1{+}1]^{(j)}=[n_1{+}1]\,[n_1]^{(j-1)}$ and
$[n_1]^{(j)}=[n_1]^{(j-1)}[n_1{-}j{+}1]$, the matching condition is
\begin{equation}
c_j=[n_1{+}1]-q^{\,j}\,[n_1{-}j{+}1]
=\frac{(1-q^{n_1+1})-q^j(1-q^{n_1-j+1})}{1-q}=[j]_q ,
\end{equation}
independent of $n_1$. Lowering sector ($|G-j-1\rangle$): the left side
receives the two close terms of \eqref{eq:TM},
$[n_1]^{(j+1)}q^{\Delta(n_1-1-j)}+q^{\Delta+n_1}[n_0][n_1]^{(j)}q^{\Delta(n_1-j)}$;
on the right, $T\,\cl_j$ contributes
$q^{\Delta+j}[n_1]^{(j)}\big([n_1{-}j]+q^{\,n_1-j}[n_0]\big)q^{\Delta(n_1-j)}$
(using $[G-j]=[n_1{-}j]+q^{\,n_1-j}[n_0]$) and $\cl_{j+1}$ contributes
$d_j\,[n_1]^{(j+1)}q^{\Delta(n_1-j-1)}$. The $[n_0]$ terms cancel
identically, and with $[n_1]^{(j+1)}=[n_1]^{(j)}[n_1{-}j]$ the remaining
condition is $q^{-\Delta}=q^{\Delta+j}+d_j\,q^{-\Delta}$, i.e.
$d_j=1-q^{2\Delta+j}$, again independent of $n_1$ (and of $n_0$).
\end{proof}

Iterating \eqref{eq:rec} from $\cl_0$, every application of $T_M$ replaces
$\cl_j$ by a combination of $\cl_{j'}$ with prefactors that are scalars or
scalar multiples of $T$ acting on the left. Since
$\cl_{j}\circ\op=\delta_{j0}$, projecting onto $j=0$ after $m$ steps gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;C(m)=P_m(T)\;}
\end{equation}
with $P_m$ the \emph{Motzkin-path polynomial}: sum over paths
$0\to0$ of length $m$ in the auxiliary height $j$, a flat step at height
$j$ contributing $q^{\Delta+j}\,T$, a down step $[j]_q$, an up step
$1-q^{2\Delta+j}$. Equivalently, $\sum_m P_m(T)\,t^m$ is the $(0,0)$
resolvent of the auxiliary tridiagonal operator --- the continued fraction
\begin{equation}
\sum_{m\ge0}P_m(T)\,t^m=
\cfrac{1}{1-t\,q^{\Delta}T-
 \cfrac{t^2\,(1-q^{2\Delta})[1]}{1-t\,q^{\Delta+1}T-
  \cfrac{t^2\,(1-q^{2\Delta+1})[2]}{1-\cdots}}} \; .
\end{equation}
The first cases are
\begin{equation}
C(0)=1,\qquad
C(1)=q^{\Delta}\,T,\qquad
C(2)=q^{2\Delta}\,T^2+\big(1-q^{2\Delta}\big),
\end{equation}
and from \eqref{eq:Ahat}
\begin{equation}
\widehat A_1=-(1-q^{\Delta})\,T,
\qquad
\widehat A_2=(1-q^{\Delta})^2\,T^2+\big(1-q^{2\Delta}\big).
\end{equation}
$\widehat A_1\propto T$ is the $N=\infty$ image of the finite-$N$ operator
identity $A_1=-2pH$ (exact per disorder instance); $\widehat A_2$ is, to
our knowledge, the first explicit higher conserved charge of the tower.

\section{Corollaries and consequences}

\begin{corollary}[what propagates and what cannot]
A random matter operator $O_\Delta$ (independent Gaussian couplings)
inserts $q^{\Delta\hat n}$, which does not commute with $T$; its channel
propagates, with $\mu_2=2(1-q^{\Delta})$. The $H$-derived bilinears insert
polynomials in $T$, so their pair amplitude cannot propagate. The
distinction is not the size of the operator but the correlation of its
couplings with those of $H$.
\end{corollary}

\section{The full channel}

Theorem~1 concerns the pair amplitude. With $X_i=\psi_i(\ad_H)^n\psi_i$
the physical correlator is
\begin{equation}
\langle A_nA_n\rangle=N\,\langle X_iX_i\rangle+N(N-1)\,\langle X_iX_j\rangle
\qquad(i\neq j),
\end{equation}
and the per-pair amplitude is smaller than the diagonal one by exactly
the $1/N$ that the multiplicity restores. The two terms are therefore of
the same order, and Theorem~1 removes one of two comparable
contributions. On a double-scaling line each term is $O(\sqrt N)$ in the
normalization $\langle H^2\rangle=1$, and the two cancel identically at
that order: the pair channel's double-scaling constant,
$\lim|\mu_2^{\rm pair}|\,pN=4\lambda(1-q)^2(5+3q)$ at $n=2$, is the
$\Delta_\psi$-slope of the diagonal channel. The physical channel is the
$O(1)$ remainder, which belongs to neither half. Evaluated exactly at
finite $N$ (Wick resummation, no diagonalization), the full $A_2$ channel
has $\mu_2/\mu_0=1.044,\,1.118,\,1.164,\,1.194,\,\dots,\,1.286$ at
$(p,N)=(4,16),(6,36),(8,64),(10,100),\dots,(40,1600)$ along
$\lambda=2$, while at $n=1$ the two parts cancel to $3\times10^{-16}$, as
the identity $A_1=-2pH$ requires.

The remainder is computed by summing the flavor first. For even $p$,
$\psi_iH\psi_i=H-2H_i$, with $H_i$ the part of $H$ whose monomials
contain flavor $i$, and $\sum_iH_i=pH$. Expanding the double commutator
then gives, for every $N$ and every disorder instance,
\begin{equation}
A_1=-2pH,\qquad A_2=4\,\Omega,\qquad \Omega=\sum_iH_i^2
=\sum_{c,c'}|c\cap c'|\,J_cJ_{c'}\,\Gamma_c\Gamma_{c'},
\label{eq:A2}
\end{equation}
with $\Gamma_c=i^{p/2}\psi_{c}$ the Hamiltonian monomial on the $p$-subset
$c$. So $A_2$ is the word $H^2$ with its two monomials weighted by the
number $X$ of indices they share. In the double-scaled limit the
pairwise intersections of the index sets of distinct chords are
independent Poisson($\mu$) variables, $\mu=\lambda/2$, and a crossing of
two chords carries $(-1)^X$, whose average is $q$. A weighted pair of
chords therefore carries $E[X(-1)^{sX}]=\mu$ or $-\mu q$ according as
the chords do not ($s=0$) or do ($s=1$) cross, and a pair weighted by
both insertions of a two-point word carries $E[X^2(-1)^{sX}]=\mu+\mu^2$
or $q(\mu^2-\mu)$. A contraction of the two slots of one insertion with
each other is the $c$-number $\langle\Omega\rangle=p$, removed by mean
subtraction. Every full-channel word is then a finite matching sum, and
\begin{equation}
\mu_0=4\lambda\big[\lambda(1+q)+2(1-q)\big],\qquad
\mu_2=16\lambda(1-q)\big[1-q^2+2\lambda q\big],
\end{equation}
so $\mu_0=32$ and $\mu_2/\mu_0=1.316906$ at $\lambda=2$, the limit of
the finite-$N$ series above.

The mean-subtracted channel still contains conserved weight, because
$A_2$ overlaps $H^2$:
\begin{equation}
\langle A_2(H^2-1)\rangle=2\lambda(1-q),\qquad
\langle(H^2-1)^2\rangle=1+q .
\end{equation}
Once that overlap is removed, what is left is an ordinary matter chord.

\begin{theorem}[the full $A_2$ channel]
At strict double scaling, for every $\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}
A_2-\langle A_2\rangle=\frac{2\lambda(1-q)}{1+q}\,(H^2-1)+\nu\,O_2,
\qquad
\nu^2=\frac{8\lambda\,(1-q^2+2\lambda q)}{1+q},
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
where $O_2$ is a unit-normalized matter chord of dimension $\Delta=2$,
uncorrelated with every function of $H$. Equivalently, with
$\tilde\Omega=\Omega-\langle\Omega\rangle$ and $G_2(a,b)$ the two-point
word $\langle H^aO_2H^bO_2\rangle$ of a $\Delta=2$ matter chord, for all
$a,b\ge0$
\begin{equation}
\langle H^a\tilde\Omega H^b\tilde\Omega\rangle
=\Big[\frac{\mu(1-q)}{1+q}\Big]^2\big\langle H^{a+b}(H^2-1)^2\big\rangle
+\frac{\nu^2}{16}\,G_2(a,b).
\label{eq:words}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sort the chord diagrams of $\langle H^a\tilde\Omega H^b\tilde\Omega\rangle$
into two classes.

\emph{Class I: both chords of one insertion end on the other.} The two
chords form a bundle, and every $H$-chord separating the insertions
crosses both strands, weighing $q^2$. The bundle itself weighs
$(\mu+\mu^2)$ nested plus $q(\mu^2-\mu)$ crossed. The class sums to
$[\mu(1-q)+\mu^2(1+q)]\,G_2(a,b)$.

\emph{Class II: they do not.} At each insertion, exchanging which of its
two adjacent slots carries which chord is an involution of the class.
No node lies between adjacent slots, so the exchange flips the mutual
crossing of the two chords and changes no other crossing; it maps the
weight $\mu$ to $-\mu q$. On the plain word $H^2$ the same exchange maps
$1$ to $q$. The two involutions commute. Summing each orbit, class II
equals $[\mu(1-q)/(1+q)]^2$ times class II of
$\langle H^a(H^2-1)H^b(H^2-1)\rangle$ (in which $H^2-1$ is exactly the
two-slot insertion without self-contraction).

Class I of that plain word is $(1+q)\,G_2(a,b)$. Adding and subtracting
it gives \eqref{eq:words} with the coefficient
$\mu(1-q)+\mu^2(1+q)-\mu^2(1-q)^2/(1+q)
=\lambda(1-q^2+2\lambda q)/\big(2(1+q)\big)=\nu^2/16$. Multiplying by
$16$, since $A_2=4\Omega$, gives \eqref{eq:split}.
\end{proof}

The only inputs are the identity \eqref{eq:A2} and the Poisson limit; no
property of the $q$-Hermite polynomials is used. Two consequences follow:
\begin{equation}
\mu_0=\underbrace{\frac{4\lambda^2(1-q)^2}{1+q}}_{\text{conserved}}
+\underbrace{\nu^2}_{\text{dynamical}},\qquad
\frac{\mu_2}{\nu^2}=2(1-q^2).
\end{equation}
The second is the matter-chord law $\mu_2=2(1-q^\Delta)$ at $\Delta=2$,
and every higher moment of the propagating part is the corresponding
$\Delta=2$ value. At $\lambda=2$, $32.9\%$ of $\mu_0$ is conserved and
the propagating width is $1.963369$; the ratio $1.316906$ above is that
width diluted. The propagating spectral density is the $\Delta=2$
matter-chord continuum \cite{BM}: smooth, with no discrete lines.

Two checks do not use the proof. The exact finite-$N$ rational moments,
with the $H^2$ component removed in exact arithmetic, extrapolate in
$1/p$ to $\mu_2/\nu^2=1.96336$ on $\lambda=2$ and $1.7293$ on
$\lambda=1$, against $1.963369$ and $1.729329$. Dense exact
diagonalization with the full commutant of $H$ projected out per
realization gives $2.03$, $2.03$, $2.01$ at $p=6$, $N=16,18,20$, against
$2(1-q^2)=2.00$; the identity-subtracted ratio there is $0.29$--$0.41$.

\section{Higher levels}

The identity \eqref{eq:A2} is the first case of a general one. For a set
$T$ of slots let $W_T=\sum_i\prod_{t=1}^n(H_i\text{ if }t\in T\text{,
else }H)$. Then, for every $n\ge2$, $N$ and even $p$,
\begin{equation}
A_n=\sum_{T\subseteq[n],\,|T|\ge2}(-2)^{|T|}\,(-1)^{n-\max T}
\binom{n-1}{\max T-1}\,W_T .
\end{equation}
It follows because $S(Y)=\sum_i\psi_iY\psi_i$ multiplies a Clifford
monomial of grade $g$ by $(-1)^g(N-2g)$, and products of the even
Hamiltonian monomials have even grade; the grade of a product of Hamiltonian
monomials is an inclusion--exclusion sum of their intersections, and
$A_n=\sum_j(-1)^j\binom{n}{j}S(H^{n-j})H^j$; it has been checked
against dense matrices for $n\le5$. In the double-scaled limit triple
intersections are $O(1/p)$, so $A_n$ is assembled from the blocks
$\Omega_k=\sum_iH_iH^kH_i$, up to exactly conserved operators.

Each block computed ($k\le4$, hence $n\le6$) decomposes, modulo
polynomials in $H$, into matter chords of dimension $2,\dots,k+2$ with
polynomial dressings; for example
$\Omega_1=\frac{q}{1+q^2}\{H,\hat O_2\}+\hat O_3$. So every $A_n$ with
$n\le6$ is a polynomial in $H$ plus matter chords of dimension
$2,\dots,n$, one new chord entering at each level. This is verified,
not proved: each kernel is solved linearly from cross-correlators and
every remaining two-point word then has zero residual, symbolically in
$(\lambda,q)$ for $k\le2$ and in exact rational arithmetic at generic
points for $k=3,4$. A proof for general $n$ is open.

The new chord at each level is faint: $14.9\%$, $1.6\%$, $0.18\%$ and
$0.02\%$ of the dynamical weight of $A_3,\dots,A_6$ at $\lambda=2$, with
norm $\propto\lambda^{2\lfloor m/2\rfloor}$ at small $\lambda$. As
$\lambda\to0$ the even chords fade out of $A_3,\dots,A_6$ linearly in
$\lambda$ while the odd ones remain: $A_3$ and $A_4$ tend to $O_3$ alone
and $A_5$ to $99.5\%\,O_3+0.53\%\,O_5$. The dimensions that survive,
$2,3,5$, follow the pattern of the large-$p$ SYK bilinear spectrum
$h=2,3,5,7,\dots$ \cite{MS}. We record that as an observation on four
levels. In particular we do not identify powers of $\lambda$ in these
norms with powers of $1/p$ in the large-$p$ expansion \cite{GR}: how the
$\lambda\to0$ limit of the double-scaled theory relates to the
large-$p$ expansion at fixed $p$ is not established here.

\section{Scope}

\emph{Majorana only.} For the complex fermions of \cite{MSS2607},
$c_ic_i^\dagger=1-n_i$ replaces $\psi_i^2=1$, so the flavor sum leaves
an occupation factor between the two halves of $M_2$ instead of removing
the fermion marks. The pair statement transfers to the Dirac model; the
full channel and its CP resolution do not yet.

\emph{Fixed $p$.} The proposal places the 't~Hooft limit at fixed $p$,
$N\to\infty$ \cite{MSS2607,MSS2506}, outside strict double scaling. At
each tested even $p=2,\dots,12$ the exact finite-$N$ laws give $\mu_2/\mu_0=8p(p-2)/N+O(N^{-2})$ in units
$\langle H^2\rangle=1$, a limiting mean-square frequency $8p(p-2)J^2$ in
the coupling normalization $\langle J^2\rangle=p!J^2/N^{p-1}$. The
identity-subtracted ratio has a maximum along fixed $p$ (at $N=24$ for
$p=4$); it is the conserved $H^2$ share of $\mu_0$ changing, and with
that component removed the width is monotone.

\emph{Boost frequency is not mass.} The spectral densities above are in
the frequency conjugate to $H$, which in the proposal generates boosts.
A particle of fixed mass has a continuous boost spectrum, so relating
these continua to meson masses requires the state and time dictionary,
which is not supplied here.

\emph{Moments.} Theorem~1 states $\mu_k=0$ for $k\ge1$ in the connected
pair correlator; its weight $\mu_0$ is nonzero.

Every statement is machine-checked in the accompanying

The full channel and Theorem~2: \texttt{duality/full\_channel\_chord.py}

Theorem~1: the recursion \eqref{eq:rec} as a matrix identity for
$j\le5$; $C(m)=P_m(T)$ and $[C(m),T]=0$ for $m\le8$; and, through an
independent transfer-matrix evaluation validated against brute-force
chord-diagram enumeration, $|\mu_k|$ at $10^{-17}$ of its
pre-cancellation magnitude for $n\le5$, even $k\le6$,
$\lambda\in[0.1,4]$ and $\Delta_\psi\in[0.1,1]$. The finite-$N$ approach
to the zero is exact: at fixed $p$, $\mu_2$ of the $n=2$ pair channel

coefficient), and along $\lambda=2$ as $c_2/(pN)$ with
$c_2\to4\lambda(1-q)^2(5+3q)=32.334$. For Theorem~2: \eqref{eq:words}
symbolically on every word through ten chord endpoints, and the two
independent checks above.
